# How to Choose a Slot Game Development Company (and What It Really Costs)

**By Kshitij Kumawat, Founder — Sudonex · Last reviewed August 2026**

A slot game development company designs, builds, and certifies the reel games you see in online casinos, handling everything from the maths model and RNG to the art, sound, and front-end code. To choose one well, you match a studio's technical depth and certification track record to your budget, target markets, and launch timeline. Sounds simple. It isn't, because most of the work that decides whether a slot succeeds happens in places you can't see from a demo reel. Here's how to look under the hood.

## What a slot game development company actually delivers

A good studio hands you more than a pretty game. You're paying for a full production stack, and it helps to know the parts before you sign anything.

A complete build usually includes:

- A **math model** and PAR sheet (the document that proves your RTP, hit frequency, and volatility)
- An **RNG** integration, tested and certified
- Art, animation, and sound design
- Front-end code in **HTML5** (and often Unity for the build pipeline)
- Server integration and a remote game server (RGS) or aggregator hookup
- Certification submission and lab liaison

If a slot game developer only talks about visuals, that's a red flag. The maths is the product. The art sells it.

## The maths behind a slot (RTP, volatility, RNG)

Every slot is a spreadsheet wearing a costume. Three numbers run the show.

**RTP (return to player)** is the long-run percentage a game pays back. Most online slots sit between 94% and 97%. Regulators cap it in some markets, so your studio needs to tune RTP to where you plan to operate.

**Volatility** is how a game feels. High-volatility slots pay rarely but big; low-volatility slots drip small wins. This is a design lever, not an accident, and it should map to your audience.

**RNG (random number generator)** decides every outcome. A certified RNG is non-negotiable, and it's the first thing a testing lab will scrutinise. Good [slot game maths and RNG](/slot-game-math-rng/) work is where experienced studios earn their fee, because a model that looks fine in a demo can drift or break under real volume.

Ask any candidate to walk you through a PAR sheet they've built. If they can't, keep looking.

## Custom vs template slots

You've got two roads, and the honest answer is that both are valid depending on your goal.

**Template slots** reskin an existing math model and engine. They're cheaper and faster, and fine if you need volume or you're testing a market. The trade-off is that dozens of other operators may run the same underlying game.

**Custom slots** are built from a fresh math model up. You own the mechanic, the feel, and the IP. That matters if you're building a brand or want [progressive jackpot slots](/progressive-jackpot-slot-games/) with a mechanic nobody else has. [Custom slot game development](/custom-slot-game-development/) costs more and takes longer, but it's the only route to something genuinely yours.

Most operators I talk to start with one or two custom flagship titles and fill the library with templates. That's a sensible mix.

## How to vet a slot studio

Here's the checklist I'd use if I were hiring a studio instead of running one.

| What to check | Why it matters | What good looks like |
|---|---|---|
| Certified titles shipped | Proves the maths survives a lab | Named games, live in regulated markets |
| Math capability in-house | Outsourced maths slows everything | PAR sheets built internally |
| HTML5 performance | Mobile is most of your traffic | 60fps on mid-range phones |
| Certification experience | GLI/BMM submissions are unforgiving | Prior passes, lab relationships |
| Integration track record | Aggregator hookups can stall launches | Existing RGS or aggregator ties |
| Post-launch support | Games need tuning after release | Clear maintenance terms |

Run every candidate through those six rows. A studio that scores well on visuals but blanks on certification will cost you months later.

## Certification (GLI/BMM)

You can't launch a real-money slot in a regulated market without independent testing. **GLI** (Gaming Laboratories International) and **BMM Testlabs** are the two names you'll hear most. They verify your RNG, confirm your stated RTP matches the actual model, and check the game against each jurisdiction's rules.

This is slow and detail-heavy. A studio that's done it before knows how to prep the submission so you don't burn weeks on rework. One that hasn't will learn on your budget. Ask directly which labs they've submitted to and which markets those games run in.

## Cost and timeline

Numbers vary by scope, but here are honest ranges for [slot game development services](/slot-game-development/) as of 2026.

1. **Template reskin:** roughly 8,000 to 20,000 USD, 4 to 8 weeks. Reuses an existing model and engine.
2. **Mid-tier custom slot:** roughly 25,000 to 60,000 USD, 3 to 5 months. Fresh maths, custom art, one bonus feature.
3. **Premium custom slot:** 70,000 USD and up, 5 to 8 months. Original mechanic, multiple features, progressive jackpot, full certification.

Certification adds cost and calendar time on top, usually a few thousand dollars per market and several weeks per lab pass. Anyone quoting a fully certified custom slot in three weeks for a few thousand dollars is quoting a template, or quoting a game you don't want to ship.

## FAQ

**How much does slot game development cost?**
A template reskin runs roughly 8,000 to 20,000 USD; a mid-tier custom slot 25,000 to 60,000 USD; and a premium custom title 70,000 USD and up. Certification adds a few thousand per market. Timeline runs from about a month for templates to eight months for premium builds.

**What is custom slot game development, and do I need it?**
Custom slot game development means building the math model, mechanic, and art from scratch so you own the IP. You need it if you're building a brand or want a mechanic competitors can't copy. If you just need library volume, templates are cheaper and quicker.

**Why does HTML5 matter for slots?**
HTML5 slots run in the browser on any device without a download, which is where the vast majority of players are. It gives you one codebase across desktop and mobile and avoids app-store friction. A well-optimised HTML5 slot holds 60fps on mid-range phones.

**Can any slot game developer handle certification?**
No. Certification through GLI or BMM demands prior experience with submissions and each market's rules. Ask a candidate which labs they've passed and in which jurisdictions before you commit.
